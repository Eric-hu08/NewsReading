Each sentence in a news article always belongs to one of four argument types: Main Claim, Regular Claim, Sub-Claim and premise.
1.Main Claim: This is the core point or central claim of the essay or discussion. All other claims support or revolve around this main claim. The first sentence of a article always is main claim.
Example: In an article about healthy eating, the main claim might be "Healthy eating contributes to a longer lifespan."
2.Regular Claim: These are secondary points that directly support the main claim. They provide specific reasons or secondary claims that back up the main claim, typically at a theoretical or conceptual level.
Example: In the context of the healthy eating article, a regular claim could be "A diet rich in fruits and vegetables can reduce the risk of heart disease."
3.Sub-Claim: These are tertiary points that support the regular claims. Sub-claims further detail and expand on the regular arguments, providing additional layers of reasoning.
Example: Supporting the regular claim above, a sub-claim could be "Antioxidants in fruits and vegetables can reduce arterial plaque buildup."
4.Premise: These are specific facts, data, or examples used to support the main claim or regular claims or sub-claims. premise provides concrete proof or data to validate the claims.
Example: Supporting the sub-claim above, evidence could be "A study found that people who eat five or more servings of fruits and vegetables daily have a 20% lower incidence of arterial plaque buildup."
